  Suggested game implementation rules

  and the same law may support different actions depending on context. Track player discoveries separately from the full
  reference data. Reveal a principle only after a meaningful event or when the player chooses to inspect the Codex.

  original game content such as characters, events, dialogue, consequences, and endings. This makes the project easier to

  2. All 48 laws — concise reference
  Each entry provides an original, high-level interpretation, practical context, a caution, and a scenario seed. Use these records
  to generate game content, not to remove the need for contextual judgment.

  LAW 01 — Never Outshine the Master
  Core principle: Manage upward perception. Make decision-makers feel secure and respected while ensuring your contribution
  remains visible.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful when a senior stakeholder feels threatened by comparison. Backfires when you hide genuine
  capability or become invisible.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Recognition, status threat, insecurity, credit attribution.

  Practical application: Offer a superior a clear win while documenting your own contribution through outcomes.

  Defensive reading: Recognise when someone is asking for deference rather than better work; maintain boundaries and evidence.

  Game scenario seed: A leader presents your idea as theirs. Choose whether to correct the record publicly, reinforce the idea while
  making your role visible, or address credit privately.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Visibility, trust, reputation; delayed resentment if contribution is erased.

  LAW 02 — Never Put Too Much Trust in Friends, Learn How to Use Enemies
  Core principle: Personal affection does not guarantee competence or reliability. Evaluate people by incentives, capability, and
  behaviour, not labels.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful when assigning consequential work or choosing partners. Backfires when it becomes blanket
  cynicism or prevents genuine trust.

  Psychology / power dynamic: In-group bias, familiarity, incentives, competence.

  Practical application: Use clear commitments and accountability regardless of personal closeness.

  Defensive reading: Avoid assuming friendship equals reliability; assess evidence and make expectations explicit.

  Game scenario seed: A close ally misses a critical commitment while a former rival offers a verifiable solution. Decide whom to trust
  with the next task.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Trust, loyalty, competence, obligation.

  LAW 03 — Conceal Your Intentions
  Core principle: Control the timing and scope of disclosure so others cannot prematurely block a legitimate plan.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful in negotiation, competitive strategy, and protecting unfinished ideas. Backfires when secrecy
  undermines collaboration or becomes deception.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Information asymmetry, anticipation, strategic uncertainty.

  Practical application: Share information on a need-to-know basis while keeping affected people informed enough to act.

  Defensive reading: Ask what information is being withheld, why, and whose interests that serves.

  Game scenario seed: A colleague asks for the full plan before agreeing to support it. Decide what to disclose, defer, or make
  conditional.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Information, suspicion, alliances, trust.

  LAW 04 — Always Say Less Than Necessary
  Core principle: Use concise, deliberate communication. Avoid weakening a strong point with over-explanation or impulsive
  disclosure.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful in tense meetings and negotiation. Backfires when brevity becomes ambiguity, coldness, or
  withholding necessary context.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Attention, ambiguity, confidence signalling.

  Practical application: State the point, evidence, and request clearly; stop when the message is complete.

  Defensive reading: Do not mistake silence or confidence for competence; ask clarifying questions when needed.

  Game scenario seed: In a high-stakes review, a stakeholder challenges your work. Choose a concise evidence-led response or a
  lengthy defensive explanation.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Reputation, authority, information.

  LAW 05 — So Much Depends on Reputation—Guard It with Your Life
  Core principle: Reputation shapes how ambiguous actions are interpreted. Build it through consistent evidence and respond quickly
  to material inaccuracies.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful when credibility affects access and cooperation. Backfires when image management outruns
  substance or every criticism is treated as an attack.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Social proof, confirmation bias, credibility.

  Practical application: Build a track record and correct material misinformation with evidence rather than retaliation.

  Defensive reading: Check claims against evidence and distinguish criticism from coordinated reputational harm.

  Game scenario seed: A rumour questions your reliability before a promotion decision. Decide whether to investigate, correct the
  record, or ignore it.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Reputation, trust, visibility, evidence.

  LAW 06 — Court Attention at All Costs
  Core principle: Important work must be noticed to influence decisions; distinctiveness can help people remember it.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful in crowded environments where good work is overlooked. Backfires when spectacle displaces
  substance or attention becomes a goal in itself.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Salience, novelty, limited attention.

  Practical application: Make valuable work legible through clear stories, demonstrations, and measurable outcomes.

  Defensive reading: Look beyond confidence and spectacle to evidence, delivery, and consistency.

  Game scenario seed: Two proposals compete for executive attention: one dramatic, one evidence-rich. Decide how to present your
  work credibly.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Visibility, reputation, resources.

  LAW 07 — Get Others to Do the Work for You, but Always Take the Credit
  Core principle: The law describes leveraging others' expertise and effort while retaining status; the game should examine the ethics
  and risks of credit allocation.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful lesson: coordinate complementary expertise and make collective work visible. Exploitative
  credit-taking damages trust and future cooperation.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Coordination, attribution, reciprocity, free-riding.

  Practical application: Give fair credit, make contributions traceable, and build systems that reward collaboration.

  Defensive reading: Watch for leaders who repeatedly claim collective success and externalise blame.

  Game scenario seed: A project succeeds through several teams, but one executive claims ownership. Decide how to represent
  contributions and protect future collaboration.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Trust, reputation, loyalty, reciprocity.

  LAW 08 — Make Other People Come to You
  Core principle: Shape the conditions of engagement so discussions occur around a clear value proposition rather than constant
  pursuit.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful in negotiation and prioritisation. Backfires when it becomes needless delay, status games, or
  refusal to meet people halfway.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Scarcity, leverage, opportunity cost.

  Practical application: Create mutual value and clear terms that make engagement worthwhile.

  Defensive reading: Ask whether apparent scarcity reflects real value or manufactured pressure.

  Game scenario seed: A powerful patron delays a meeting to make you chase approval. Decide whether to pursue, offer a stronger
  reason to meet, or seek another route.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Leverage, resources, timing, reputation.

  LAW 09 — Win Through Your Actions, Never Through Argument
  Core principle: Demonstration and evidence often persuade more effectively than abstract debate.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful when disagreement is entrenched. Backfires when action is unsafe, evidence is weak, or
  dialogue is genuinely needed.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Experiential learning, confirmation bias, face-saving.

  Practical application: Run a small test, show evidence, and invite scrutiny rather than trying to dominate the argument.

  Defensive reading: Demand reproducible evidence; a polished demonstration can still be selective or misleading.

  Game scenario seed: A rival disputes your strategy. Choose between arguing, proposing a test, or negotiating a shared success
  metric.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Evidence, reputation, resources, conflict.

  LAW 10 — Infection: Avoid the Unhappy and Unlucky
  Core principle: Moods and repeated behaviour can affect teams; choose environments and relationships thoughtfully without
  reducing people to their hardships.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful for setting boundaries around chronic destructive behaviour. Backfires when it encourages
  stigma, abandonment, or blaming people for misfortune.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Emotional contagion, attribution, social influence.

  Practical application: Set boundaries around harmful conduct while offering fair support and judging actions, not circumstances.

  Defensive reading: Notice when someone uses pessimism to narrow options, but do not assume distress makes a person
  untrustworthy.

  Game scenario seed: A talented colleague repeatedly undermines every plan. Decide whether to coach, set boundaries, or change
  the working relationship.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Team climate, loyalty, reputation.

  LAW 11 — Learn to Keep People Dependent on You
  Core principle: Unique expertise or reliable value creates leverage, but deliberately manufacturing dependency creates fragility and
  resentment.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful as a reminder to build distinctive capability. Backfires when knowledge is hoarded or successors
  are blocked.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Bottlenecks, switching costs, reciprocity.

  Practical application: Become valuable by solving important problems and sharing enough knowledge for the organisation to
  succeed.

  Defensive reading: Identify single points of failure and reward knowledge-sharing rather than gatekeeping.

  Game scenario seed: A colleague withholds a critical process to protect their position. Decide whether to rely on them, document
  the process, or redesign ownership.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Leverage, resources, trust, resilience.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful for building trust through genuine openness. Backfires when a gesture is staged or followed by a

  Psychology / power dynamic: Reciprocity, trust, disarming signals.

  Practical application: Be candid about a meaningful point and generous without creating an undisclosed obligation.

  Defensive reading: Ask whether a generous act comes with pressure, a concealed condition, or a pattern of strings attached.

  Game scenario seed: A rival unexpectedly shares valuable information. Decide whether to reciprocate, verify it, or ask what they
  need.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Trust, obligation, information.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Incentives, motivation, reciprocity.

  LAW 14 — Pose as a Friend, Work as a Spy

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful lesson: listen carefully, ask thoughtful questions, and verify material information ethically.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Information gathering, impression formation, uncertainty reduction.

  Practical application: Use transparent discovery, public evidence, and respectful questions.

  Defensive reading: Notice probing questions, but do not assume every inquiry is hostile; protect confidential information

  Game scenario seed: A new ally asks detailed questions about your strategy. Decide what to share and what to verify first.

  LAW 15 — Crush Your Enemy Totally
  Core principle: Unresolved conflict can recur when root causes remain. The literal pursuit of total destruction can escalate conflict

  Psychology / power dynamic: Escalation, revenge, commitment, conflict closure.

  Practical application: Address root causes, establish enforceable boundaries, and seek proportionate resolution.

  Defensive reading: Recognise all-or-nothing framing and attempts to make compromise seem impossible.

  Game scenario seed: A rival has repeatedly blocked a project. Decide whether to negotiate a durable settlement, escalate through

  Game scenario seed: Two teams present competing proposals. Decide whether to rely on a dramatic demo, detailed evidence, or
  both.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Visibility, reputation, evidence.

  LAW 38 — Think as You Like, but Behave Like Others
  Core principle: Understanding shared norms helps ideas travel; conformity becomes harmful when it suppresses principled
  disagreement.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful when entering a new group or communicating across cultures. Backfires when it demands
  complicity or erases identity.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Norms, belonging, social acceptance.

  Practical application: Learn the local context while preserving core values and raising disagreement constructively.

  Defensive reading: Notice when conformity is used to silence dissent or excuse harmful conduct.

  Game scenario seed: You join a team where disagreement is punished. Decide how to learn the norms while raising a critical
  concern.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Trust, reputation, alliances.

  LAW 39 — Stir Up Waters to Catch Fish
  Core principle: Provocation can reveal reactions, but deliberately creating chaos often damages trust and creates collateral harm.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful defensive lesson: remain composed under provocation and verify information before reacting.
  Backfires when agitation becomes the tactic.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Emotional arousal, impulsivity, conflict escalation.

  Practical application: Keep discussions structured and slow down decisions when people are emotionally activated.

  Defensive reading: Recognise baiting, artificial urgency, and attempts to make you react before thinking.

  Game scenario seed: A rival makes a provocative accusation just before a decision. Decide whether to react, request evidence, or
  pause the discussion.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Information, reputation, authority.

  LAW 40 — Despise the Free Lunch
  Core principle: Free offers can carry hidden obligations or poor incentives; evaluate the full cost, not just the price.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful when assessing favours, gifts, and sponsorship. Backfires when it rejects genuine generosity or
  makes every relationship transactional.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Reciprocity, hidden costs, dependency.

  Practical application: Clarify terms and obligations while accepting genuine mutual aid where appropriate.

  Defensive reading: Ask what a free benefit costs in control, data, time, reputation, or future obligation.

  Game scenario seed: A patron offers valuable support with no written terms. Decide whether to accept, clarify expectations, or
  decline.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Resources, obligation, leverage.

  LAW 41 — Avoid Stepping into a Great Man's Shoes
  Core principle: Successors need a distinct contribution rather than relying entirely on a predecessor's identity or reputation.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful during leadership transitions. Backfires when differentiation becomes disrespect or needless
  rejection of what works.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Social comparison, identity, legitimacy.

  Practical application: Respect inherited strengths while establishing a credible direction of your own.

  Defensive reading: Notice when a successor is judged against an idealised predecessor or uses comparison to undermine others.

  Game scenario seed: You replace a celebrated leader. Decide what to preserve, what to change, and how to establish your own
  credibility.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Reputation, authority, identity.

  LAW 42 — Strike the Shepherd and the Sheep Will Scatter
  Core principle: A conflict may be driven by a central organiser; address the root cause rather than treating every participant as the
  enemy.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful for resolving coordinated disruption. Backfires when it scapegoats a leader or punishes a group
  without evidence.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Coordination, network centrality, group dynamics.

  Practical application: Identify the real source of the problem, use fair process, and avoid collective punishment.

  Defensive reading: Check whether someone is falsely identifying a scapegoat to divide a group.

  Game scenario seed: A team-wide resistance appears to centre on one influential person. Decide whether to investigate, engage
  them, or address broader causes.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Relationships, information, authority.

  LAW 43 — Work on the Hearts and Minds of Others
  Core principle: Durable influence comes from understanding people's concerns and treating them as participants, not obstacles.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful for change leadership and coalition-building. Backfires when empathy is performed to
  manipulate agreement.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Empathy, identity, reciprocity, participation.

  Practical application: Listen, incorporate legitimate concerns, and make the reasoning behind decisions visible.

  Defensive reading: Distinguish genuine listening from scripted empathy used to bypass consent.

  Game scenario seed: A team resists a major change. Decide whether to impose it, explain it, or redesign it with affected people.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Loyalty, trust, reputation.

  LAW 44 — Disarm and Infuriate with the Mirror Effect
  Core principle: Reflecting language, concerns, or behaviour can build rapport or reveal patterns, but mimicry can feel mocking or
  manipulative.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful for active listening and surfacing inconsistencies. Backfires when used to provoke or humiliate.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Mirroring, self-recognition, emotional response.

  Practical application: Reflect the substance of what someone says and check whether you understood correctly.

  Defensive reading: Notice when someone mirrors you excessively to manufacture intimacy or provoke a reaction.

  Game scenario seed: A negotiator repeats your own words to pressure you. Decide whether to engage with the substance, name
  the tactic, or reset the conversation.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Trust, information, negotiation.

  LAW 45 — Preach the Need for Change, but Never Reform Too Much at Once
  Core principle: Change is easier to sustain when people can absorb it and see continuity with what they value.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful for transformations involving many stakeholders. Backfires when gradualism preserves serious
  harm or delays urgent action.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Status quo bias, change fatigue, identity.

  Practical application: Sequence change, explain what remains stable, and move faster when risk or evidence requires it.

  Defensive reading: Watch for calls for gradualism that indefinitely postpone necessary action.

  Game scenario seed: You inherit a failing process. Decide whether to replace it immediately, stage the transition, or run a parallel
  pilot.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Resources, reputation, loyalty.

  LAW 46 — Never Appear Too Perfect
  Core principle: Visible humility and acknowledgement of effort can reduce envy and make success relatable; manufactured flaws
  can appear inauthentic.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful when success creates distance. Backfires when leaders downplay achievements or use staged
  vulnerability.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Social comparison, envy, authenticity.

  Practical application: Share credit, acknowledge uncertainty, and remain open to correction without diminishing real achievement.

  Defensive reading: Do not assume a polished person is dishonest; assess whether their confidence allows scrutiny.

  Game scenario seed: After a major win, colleagues become distant. Decide how to communicate success, credit the team, and
  invite feedback.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Reputation, loyalty, visibility.

  LAW 47 — Know When to Stop After Victory
  Core principle: A successful move can become a failure when ambition continues beyond the original objective. Define success and
  exit conditions.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful after a negotiation, launch, or conflict. Backfires when stopping too early leaves essential risks
  unresolved.

  Psychology / power dynamic: Escalation of commitment, overconfidence, diminishing returns.

  Practical application: Set a target, assess the new situation, and stop or adapt when further action adds more risk than value.

  Defensive reading: Notice when someone keeps expanding demands after the agreed goal has been met.

  Game scenario seed: You have won a key concession. Decide whether to secure the agreement, demand more, or protect the
  gains already made.
  Suggested mechanic tags: Timing, resources, ambition, reputation.

  LAW 48 — Assume Formlessness
  Core principle: Adapt tactics to changing conditions instead of becoming trapped by one identity, strategy, or structure.

  Useful when / failure mode: Useful in volatile environments. Backfires when adaptability becomes inconsistency or lack of

  Design tension examples
  Visibility vs. restraint: becoming noticed may improve access but can provoke status threats. Trust vs. information
  control: protecting sensitive plans may preserve options but damage collaboration. Boldness vs. timing: acting decisively
  may create momentum but can lock the player into a weak position. Adaptability vs. consistency: changing tactics can
  improve fit but undermine trust if commitments also shift.

  Use such tensions to create dilemmas with no universally correct answer. Make the best choice depend on
  character motives, available evidence, current meters, prior choices, and the player's objective.

  5. Content authoring and validation checklist
  Use this checklist for every new event card.

  ■ The situation is specific and understandable in a few seconds.

  ■ Both choices are plausible; neither is an obviously correct answer in every context.

  ■ Immediate effects and delayed effects are distinct and testable.

  ■ Any character knowledge or secret referenced by the narrative has a valid source in state.

  ■ The event has clear eligibility conditions and cannot repeat accidentally.

  ■ The consequences reflect character motives, relationships, and prior actions.

  ■ The debrief is concise, original, and framed as a contextual interpretation rather than a universal rule.

  ■ Any source law title or number has been verified against the supplied book before being labelled as exact.

  ■ The event is original and does not reproduce the book's anecdotes, paragraphs, or distinctive expression.

  ■ The event has been tested for both choice paths and any follow-up chain.


  Suggested content validation script
  Create a script that checks unique IDs, required fields, valid character and event references, legal effect types, valid law IDs,
  reachable follow-up events, and whether at least one fallback event is always eligible. Run this validation before the game
  starts in development mode and in automated tests.

  Source-verification checklist
  When the book PDF is available, verify each exact law title, number, and any source-specific nuance. Store the verification
  status and optional page locator in project metadata. Do not place copied source passages in the public game's data files.

  6. Final implementation brief
  Build the game as a modular HTML5 experience with a swipe-based decision loop, four power meters, recurring characters,
  hidden agendas, relationship state, delayed consequences, secret objectives, multiple endings, local save/load, and a Codex.

  Use this reference to connect each event to one or more strategic themes. Do not force every event to illustrate exactly one
  law. Some of the most interesting decisions should create conflicts between laws, values, and short- versus long-term goals.

  The game should teach the player to read incentives, reputation, timing, uncertainty, alliances, and manipulation. It should
  also teach the player to recognise when these tactics are being used against them. Do not reward cruelty as the only viable
  strategy; trust, fair dealing, restraint, and cooperation should sometimes outperform deception.

  Acceptance criteria
  Claude Code should produce a structured law dataset; validate exact source titles against the separately supplied PDF;
  connect law IDs and themes to original events; create contextual debriefs; and ensure the full game runs without requiring a
  remote AI service.
